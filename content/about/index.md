# **Computational Healthcare & Oncology Informatics (CHOI) Lab**

Welcome to the **Computational Healthcare & Oncology Informatics (CHOI) Lab**, located within the Department of Radiation Oncology at the Sidney Kimmel Medical College, Thomas Jefferson University.

Led by **[Wookjin Choi, Ph.D.](/profile/)**, our lab operates at the intersection of medical physics, computer vision, and oncology. We develop interpretable machine learning models, quantitative radiomics pipelines, and advanced image analysis tools to enhance cancer diagnosis, optimize radiation therapy planning, and predict patient outcomes.

---

## **Laboratory Mission**

Our mission is to translate computational innovations into actionable clinical tools. We focus on:
1. **Precision Oncology:** Designing workflows that personalize cancer treatment plans based on quantitative imaging features.
2. **Explainable AI (XAI):** Rejecting "black-box" models in favor of interpretable algorithms that clinicians can understand, troubleshoot, and trust.
3. **Reproducibility:** Publishing open-source, standardized software packages (such as our flagship [QRadiomics CLI](/qradiomics/)) under the [CHOI Lab GitHub Organization](https://github.com/choilab-jefferson) so our research findings can be independently verified by the global scientific community.

---

## **Core Research Areas**

### **1. Cardiotoxicity Prediction (Cardiac PET Radiomics)**
As lung cancer survival rates improve, the long-term cardiac toxicities of thoracic radiation therapy have become a major clinical concern. We utilize functional delta-radiomics on 18F-FDG PET/CT scans to quantify cardiac uptake and predict future radiation-induced heart damage, allowing early intervention before symptoms become debilitating.

### **2. Lung Nodule Characterization & Screening**
Using public datasets like LIDC-IDRI and LUNGx, we develop shape-based feature descriptors (including spiculation and lobulation metrics) to quantify early lung nodule malignancy. Our tools enable automated nodule characterization, bridging the gap between radiologists' visual assessments and computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) systems.

### **3. MR-Guided Adaptive Radiotherapy (MRI-Linac)**
We develop real-time image processing and motion-tracking algorithms for next-generation MRI-guided radiation therapy systems, ensuring precise target volume delineation and minimizing exposure to healthy surrounding organs-at-risk.
